# Are LLMs Solving Math?

Are LLM agents solving all known math problems from low to high complexity consistently now or are LLMs "only" synthesizing solutions for ripe problems given humanities current math knowledge (by synthesing existing knowledge via analogy - running experiments at massive scale)?

LLM intelligence is opposite to how we normally think of (human) intelligence: very grounded, methological thinking based on first pricinples. It is like having someone who knows a lot but is slightly drunk throwing out ideas. But with enough guidance and verification you can extract useful output. - paraphrased from Terence Tao interview.

I can see LLMs marching on to solving math only by creating a loop of continual learning - where experiments produce (verified) knowledge that is used to guide the next round of experiments.

## Value of human understanding

Tao also emphasizes that LLM written proofs show lack of human understanding: the proofs stretch trivial (uninteresting) parts too long and keep the most interesting parts too short. LLMs lacking this ability, may indicate that truly autonomous AI scientists are still further away than AI labs like to think..

>I appreciate automated theorem proving probably more than the average person, but the below is ridiculously ignorant -- pure engagement farming.
>
>Somehow we're supposed to believe that the field of mathematics has made more progress in 2026 than in the 140 years prior... In reality our understanding of mathematics is nearly unchanged compared to 2025, while the 140 prior years saw the development of axiomatic set theory, category theory, algebraic topology, game theory, information theory, chaos theory, all modern statistics, etc...
>
>Math is just night and day if you compare 1880 to 2025. What new insights have we produced in 2026 that exceed that? Ever wonder where those "open problems in math" come from?
- [François Chollet on X](https://x.com/fchollet/status/2126429852295463382)

## Artificial Super Intelligence

A good indicator for ASI:
Every new model release should come with more and more and **harder** open math problems solved (assuming ASI is rly incoming). If that is not the case recent models only solved problems that were ripe (enough previous human work to start from). Let’s see…

Does every new generation of AI models independently push the boundary of mathematical knowledge further than the previous generation, at an accelerating rate? If yes: ASI is incoming, if not: we are in a capability plateau. The next months will paint the picture.
